— Section 03 / Night Shift

The night shift.

Most of our installations happen between 9pm and 5am — when the building is empty, the residents are away, and the fitters can work without disruption. A log of three recent shifts.

23:40 · MAR 14

Penthouse balcony — Hebbal

Four balconies on the 18th floor. Wind was a steady 15 knots. Net tensioned by 02:15, anchors sealed by 03:00. The resident arrived home at 07:30 and did not notice the fitting.

21:00 · FEB 28

Duplex atrium — Sadashivanagar

A six-metre span across a double-height living room. Scaffolding assembled at 19:00, net in place by midnight, scaffolding struck by 03:00. The family slept through it.

22:30 · JAN 17

Staircase well — Indiranagar

Three floors of open staircase. Child-safety net in 40mm nylon, 48 anchors drilled into limestone walls. The dog was the only one who noticed — he watched from the landing for two hours.

Issue 01 · Spring The Safety-Net Atelier Bangalore · 2024

Woven, not built.

A safety net is the last piece of architecture — the textile that holds the void. Each piece is composed of three materials, drawn from three origins, and fitted by one hand. This is the journal of that work.

Open the atlas→
— Section 01 / Material Atlas

Tap a material. Read its story.

Every net is made from six raw materials. Each one has an origin, a tensile strength, and a story. Tap any card to read it — the way you'd open a swatch book in a tailor's atelier.

Material 01

HDPE

High-Density Polyethylene

Origin
Petroleum resin · extruded to 0.3mm twine
Tensile
120–300 kg
UV Resistance
Stabilised at extrusion
Used in
Bird nets · roof nets · pet nets

The workhorse. The same polymer used in marine rope — drawn fine, twisted three-ply, and UV-stabilised at the extrusion stage so the protection runs through the core, not just the surface.

Material 02

Nylon 6

Polyamide 6

Origin
Polymerised caprolactam · 0.5mm twine
Tensile
250–600 kg
UV Resistance
Coated after weaving
Used in
Child safety · atrium nets · fall arrest

The polymer that holds. Stronger than HDPE by a factor of two, softer to the touch, and elastic enough to absorb the shock of a falling body without snapping. The choice for anything that must hold a person.

Material 03

Polyester

PET filament · woven fine

Origin
Extruded PET · drawn to 0.1mm
Tensile
40–80 kg
UV Resistance
Inherent
Used in
Mosquito mesh · insect screen

The finest weave. A filament thinner than a human hair, woven tight enough to stop a mosquito. Framed in aluminium, it disappears into the window it protects — the room breathes, the insect doesn't enter.

Material 04

SS 316L

Stainless Steel 316L

Origin
Austenitic steel · molybdenum-alloyed
Tensile
≥ 800 kg per anchor
Corrosion
Marine-grade · salt-resistant
Used in
All anchors · perimeter fixing

What holds the net to the wall. 316L — the L for low carbon, which means it won't crack at the weld. Drilled 60mm into masonry, torque-sealed, and individually logged. The net is only as strong as the anchor.

Material 05

UV Coating

Warm-polymer bath

Origin
Sailcloth marine coating · adapted
Performance
30-year colourfast
Salt Resistance
Coastal-rated
Used in
All exterior nets

The bath the fibre drinks. A warm-polymer coating originally developed for sailcloth — UV-stabilised, salt-resistant, colourfast for three decades. The net that leaves the bath is not the net that entered it.

Material 06

Border Rope

Polypropylene · 6mm

Origin
Extruded PP · braided 12-strand
Tensile
400 kg
Function
Edge tension distribution
Used in
All perimeter edges

The edge that holds the mesh. A 6mm braided rope sewn into the perimeter of every net — distributes the load from mesh to anchor, so no single point carries the whole. The border is the net's handshake with the wall.

— Section 02 / Field Journal

Three installations.

A field journal of three recent commissions — the balcony, the staircase, the atrium. Each one a different problem, a different mesh, a different hand on the tensioning jig.

Entry 01 · The Balcony

A pigeon problem on the fourteenth floor.

The resident had tried spikes, reflective tape, and an ultrasonic device. The pigeons ignored all three. We fitted 25mm HDPE in two hours — the birds left by sunset and have not returned in three years. The net is invisible from the living room.

"The birds left by sunset. The net is invisible from the sofa."

Entry 02 · The Staircase

A toddler who learned to open the gate.

The parents had a stair gate. The child learned to open it at nineteen months. We fitted 40mm nylon across the mezzanine — 250kg tensile, soft to the touch, and tensioned drum-taut. The child presses against it every morning. It gives, then returns.

"It gives, then returns. That is what a safety net is for."

Entry 03 · The Atrium

A void in the centre of the house.

A duplex apartment with a six-metre atrium — the architect's centrepiece, the family's anxiety. We fitted a 45mm nylon net across the span, edge-bound in PP rope, anchored every 50cm into the limestone. From below, it reads as a faint shadow against the skylight.

"From below, it reads as a faint shadow against the skylight."

— Section 03 / The Atelier

The bench in Peenya where every net begins.

Our workshop sits in Peenya, Bangalore's industrial estate. Three benches, six fitters, one tensioning jig. Every net is cut, edge-bound, and pre-tensioned on the bench before it travels to site.

The bench is the important part. A net that leaves the bench untensioned will never be tensioned on the wall. So we tension twice — once on the bench, once on the building — and the net does not leave until both readings match.
